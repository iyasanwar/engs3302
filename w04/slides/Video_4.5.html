From two classes to many

A score for each class

gᵢ(x) = P(ωᵢ | x)gᵢ(x) = p(x | ωᵢ) P(ωᵢ)gᵢ(x) = ln p(x | ωᵢ) + ln P(ωᵢ)

Two classes, as in Videos 4.1 and 4.2: compute both posteriors and take the larger. At x = 5.4: sea bass.

Three classes: one posterior for each class, and the same rule: take the largest.

Each posterior is a score for its class: a discriminant function gᵢ(x).

Three scores give the same decisions: dropping the evidence or taking the log never changes which is largest.

Start from what we did with two classes: at 5.4 the posteriors 0.526 and 0.474, take the larger, sea bass. Click 1: three classes, three posteriors, the same rule: the largest. Click 2: each posterior is a score for its class, a discriminant function gᵢ(x). Click 3: posterior, likelihood times prior, or the log of it: the same winner. The log turns a product into a sum and avoids tiny numbers. For minimum risk, the score is minus the risk.

Two pulls on one decision

g(x) = ln [p(x | ω₁) / p(x | ω₂)] + ln [P(ω₁) / P(ω₂)]
measurement: ln(0.111/0.150) = −0.30prior: ln(0.6/0.4) = +0.405g = +0.105 > 0: sea bass

Gain 2: each part of g is a pull. A negative pull is towards salmon, a positive pull towards sea bass.

The measurement: at 5.4 a salmon is more likely to look like this. It pulls towards salmon.

The prior: sea bass are more common. It pulls towards sea bass, a little harder.

The sum is small and positive: sea bass, but only just. That is why the posterior was 0.526, close to one half.

Gain 2. Click 1: the measurement part at 5.4 is minus 0.30: towards salmon. Click 2: the prior part is plus 0.405: towards sea bass. Click 3: the sum is plus 0.105: sea bass, only just; the two pulls nearly cancel, which is why the posterior was 0.526.

One function gives the score of each class. Dividing by the sum gives the posteriors.

argmax: the class with the largest score. That is the minimum-error decision.

argmin of loss @ posteriors: the action with the smallest risk. The same fish, a different answer.

The whole week in a few lines. Click 1: scores and posteriors at 5.4: 0.526 and 0.474. Click 2: argmax at 5.2 gives 1, salmon. Click 3: the loss matrix times the posteriors gives the risks; argmin gives 0, sea bass. You will run this in the lab.

Remember

Choose the largest posterior, or the smallest risk

Posterior = likelihood × prior / evidence. Choose the largest: the fewest mistakes.

The mistakes are two areas. The Bayes boundary makes them smallest. The prior moves it.

With a loss table, choose the smallest risk. It can make more mistakes but cost less.
