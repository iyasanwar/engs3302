From P(light | sea bass) to P(sea bass | light)

Turning a conditional around

We know how each class looks: P(light | sea bass). The classifier needs the reverse: P(sea bass | light).

P(A | B) = P(B | A) · P(A)P(B)
Bayes’ rule

Each branch carries a probability. Multiply along a path: 0.60 × 0.933 = 0.56, the light sea bass.

A fish is light in two ways: a light sea bass, or a light salmon. Total probability:

P(light) =0.933×0.60+0.075×0.40=0.59

Keep the light fish only. The sea bass part of them:

P(sea bass | light) =0.56÷0.59=0.949

The same as counting: 56 of the 59 light fish. So why a rule? Next slide.

Click 1: the tree: 0.60 sea bass, 0.40 salmon; 0.933 of the sea bass are light, 0.075 of the salmon. Click 2: total probability: P(light) = 0.933 × 0.60 + 0.075 × 0.40 = 0.56 + 0.03 = 0.59. Click 3: Bayes: P(sea bass | light) = 0.56 / 0.59 = 0.949. Click 4: the same as 56 of 59 in the table.

Why we need the rule

What stays, what changes

stays

0.933 of sea bass are light. This describes the fish.

stays

0.075 of salmon are light. This describes the fish.

changes

The share of each species in the catch. It changes with the season, the place and the net.

So we measure the first two numbers once. On a new day we only need the new shares, not a new table. Bayes’ rule puts them together.

If we can count, why the rule? The rule needs how sea bass look (0.933), how salmon look (0.075), and the share of each species. The first two describe the fish, so we assume they stay the same from day to day. The shares change with season, place and net. Click 2: measure the two once; on a new day only the shares are new.

Same fish, a different catch

When sea bass are rare

Another day. The fish look the same: 0.933 and 0.075. But only 5% of the catch is sea bass.

47 / 118 ≈ 0.40

Think of 1,000 fish: 50 sea bass (blue), 950 salmon.

Light ones: about 47 of the 50 sea bass, and about 71 of the 950 salmon.

Group the light fish: 118. Only 47 of them are sea bass.

Bayes, no 1,000 fish needed:
0.933 × 0.05 = 0.0467;  0.0467 + 0.075 × 0.95 = 0.1180

0.0467÷0.1180=0.396

The fish is light, yet it is probably a salmon. Each salmon is rarely light, but there are so many salmon. How common each class is changes the answer.

Another day: the fish look the same (0.933 and 0.075) but sea bass are 5% of the catch. Click 1: of 1,000 fish, about 47 light sea bass and 71 light salmon. Click 2: group the light ones: 118, only 47 sea bass: about 0.40. Click 3: the rule: 0.0467 / 0.1180 = 0.396. Click 4: light, yet probably a salmon. Week 4 names these prior and posterior.
