Two features

Three cases, three shapes

Σ₁ = Σ₂ = σ²Iround, same size → straight: nearest mean
Σ₁ = Σ₂ = Σsame tilted shape → straight: LDA
Σ₁ ≠ Σ₂each its own shape → curved: QDA
Now.So.Next.

Each class: a mean vector and a covariance matrix Σᵢ = the shape of class i. Three possible shapes.

Round clouds of the same size: a straight line. With equal priors, this is the nearest mean of Week 2.

The same tilted shape for both: still a straight line. This is LDA, linear discriminant analysis.

Each class its own shape: the x² terms stay, and the boundary bends. This is QDA.

Now.So.Next.

The 342 penguins, by bill length and bill depth. Each species is a tilted cloud.

LDA: one shared shape for all three species. The boundaries are straight lines.

QDA: each species keeps its own shape. The lines bend a little (dashed: LDA).

On the 103 test penguins: LDA 99, QDA 101. Two penguins is a small difference: another split often ties.

The tilts differ: Adelie 0.39, Chinstrap 0.65, Gentoo 0.64. So the shapes really differ. This fits QDA's assumption.

Now.So.Next.

QDA won by two penguins. At what price? Every number in the model is estimated from training points.

In QDA, each species has its own matrix: d variances and d(d − 1)/2 covariances. 100 features, 3 species: 15150.

LDA: one matrix for all three species, estimated from all the points together. A third of QDA.

Naive Bayes: only the d variances of each species. QDA follows each shape, but needs far more data.

15150 shape numbers for QDA with 100 features and 3 species. What is the simplest shape we could assume?
