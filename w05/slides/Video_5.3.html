The score of a normal class

gᵢ(x) = −(x − μᵢ)²2σᵢ² − ln σᵢ + ln P(ωᵢ)
sea bass−½ ×1.6²+ ln0.6=−1.791
salmon−½ ×1.4²+ ln0.4=−1.896

−(x − μ)² / 2σ²
minus half the squared z-score: far from the mean, a low score

− ln σ
a penalty for a wide curve

+ ln P
a bonus for the common class

Now.So.Next.

The score of a class: the log of density × prior. The log turns the product into a sum. ωᵢ = class i

Three parts: distance, spread and prior. The constant ln √(2π) is the same for all, so we drop it.

The fish at 5.4. Sea bass: 1.6 away. Both classes have σ = 1, so − ln σ = 0.

Salmon: 1.4 away, but a smaller prior. Sea bass wins by 0.105, as in Week 4.

Now.So.Next.

Where are the two scores equal? That point is the boundary: 5.36 for our fish in Week 4.

Start halfway between the means: 5.5. With equal priors, that would be the threshold.

The prior pushes the threshold away from the common class, to give sea bass more room: 5.365.

The formula does both steps: halfway, then the shift by the prior. x* = the threshold

Why is the boundary one point? The derivation, for understanding.

same σ, so x² cancels: a straight line, one threshold.
