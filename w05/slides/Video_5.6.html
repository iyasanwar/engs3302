Week 3: one curve for the flipper of 123 Gentoo. Add the body mass: each penguin is a point, the class a cloud.

The centre of the cloud is the mean vector: one average per feature. μ = the mean vector

The spread in each direction: the two variances, on the diagonal of the covariance matrix. Σ = covariance matrix

The tilt: long flippers go with heavy bodies. The off-diagonal number, the covariance, measures it.

Now.So.Next.

Where does the covariance 2278 come from? By hand, on the first five Gentoo, with mass in kg.

Subtract the averages: how far each penguin is above or below the centre, in each feature.

Multiply the two distances. Both above or both below: positive. One above, one below: negative.

Their average, 3.20 mm·kg, is the covariance. The positive products win: the cloud tilts up.

In grams it is 1000 times larger. The correlation has no units: always from −1 to 1. ρ = correlation

We can measure the tilt. Does the tilt change how typical a new penguin is?
