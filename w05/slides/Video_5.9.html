Now.So.Next.

The simplest shape: forget the tilt. Naive Bayes: the features are independent within each class.

Then each feature gets its own Week 3 bell curve: one for bill length, one for bill depth.

The two-feature density is their product. Its ellipses are never tilted.

Features are rarely independent: that is why it is called naive. But it needs few numbers.

Now.So.Next.

What do we lose when we forget the tilt? A new penguin: bill 44 mm long, 17 mm deep.

Read the six densities: each one is a Week 3 normal curve.

Multiply prior × density × density. Naive Bayes decides Adelie, posterior 0.41.

QDA keeps the tilt and decides Chinstrap, 0.89: the point lies along the Chinstrap tilt.

Naive Bayes ignored the tilt and disagreed with QDA. What do correlated features do to naive Bayes?
