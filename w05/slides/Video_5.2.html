Why the log?

1000 fish from N(4, 1): sum of logs at μ = 4−1397
Now.So.Next.

The products are tiny, with only five fish. What happens with many fish?

1000 fish: the product is so small that the computer stores 0.0. Every guess looks the same.

The tool: the log turns a product into a sum. 1000 fish give a normal number.

The four guesses again, as sums of logs. A larger product has a larger log: the winner stays.

Now every guess, not only four: the log-likelihood is a hill. Its top is at the average.

Why exactly at the average? The derivation, for understanding.

largest likelihood = smallest sum of squares, and the average makes it smallest.

Five salmon widths

  1. μ̂ = 86.8 / 5 = 17.36; σ̂² = 1.378 (divide by 5).
  2. Σ(x − 17)² = 5 × 1.378 + 5 × 0.36² = 7.54; Σ(x − 18)² = 5 × 1.378 + 5 × 0.64² = 8.94. The smaller sum wins, so μ = 17: it is closer to 17.36.
