So far every mistake counted the same. In real systems, some mistakes are worse than others.

Duda’s fish factory: calling a sea bass “salmon” is the expensive mistake.

We write the costs in a loss table: λᵢⱼ is the loss for action αᵢ when the true class is ωⱼ.

Rows are actions, columns are true classes. Correct decisions cost 0. Bishop’s book writes it the other way round.

Some mistakes are worse. Click 1: in Duda's factory, calling a sea bass salmon costs more. Click 2: the loss table, lambda i j: the loss for action alpha i when the truth is omega j. Example 4.5

The risk of each action at x = 5.2

x = 5.2 · posteriors:0.3790.621loss:12
R(α₁ | x): decide sea bass0×0.379+1×0.621=0.621
R(α₂ | x): decide salmon2×0.379+0×0.621=0.758
minimum error: salmon (0.621)minimum risk: sea bass (0.621 < 0.758)

We do not know the true class, only its posterior. So for each action we average its losses, using the posteriors as weights.

Decide sea bass: wrong only if it is a salmon, cost 1. Risk 0.621.

Decide salmon: wrong only if it is a sea bass, cost 2. Risk 0.758.

The smaller risk is sea bass, although the fish is probably a salmon (62%).

R(αᵢ | x) = Σⱼ λᵢⱼ P(ωⱼ | x). Here we say salmon only when P(ω₂ | x) is larger than 2/3.

Worked example 4.5. Posteriors at 5.2: 0.379 and 0.621. Click 1: risk of deciding sea bass: 0 times 0.379 plus 1 times 0.621: 0.621. Click 2: deciding salmon: 2 times 0.379 plus 0: 0.758. Click 3: the smaller risk is sea bass, although minimum error would say salmon. Click 4: the conditional risk formula; with this table we say salmon only when its posterior is above 2/3.

With this loss table we decide sea bass when 2 P(ω₁ | x) > P(ω₂ | x), that is, when P(ω₁ | x) > 1/3.

The boundary moves left, to 5.13. The sea bass region grows: the costly mistake is calling a sea bass “salmon”.

Minimum error: 65.1 mistakes. Cost 2 × 30.6 + 34.5 = 95.7.

One rule inside the other

Minimum error is a special case

TRUE ω₁TRUE ω₂
α₁01
α₂10
R(α₁ | x) = 0 · P(ω₁ | x) + 1 · P(ω₂ | x) = P(ω₂ | x)R(α₂ | x) = 1 · P(ω₁ | x) + 0 · P(ω₂ | x) = P(ω₁ | x)smallest risk = largest posterior

Suppose every mistake costs 1 and every correct decision costs 0. This is the zero-one loss.

Then the risk of an action is the posterior of the other class: the chance that the action is wrong.

The smallest risk goes with the largest posterior. Minimum error is minimum risk with equal costs.

The zero-one loss: every mistake costs 1. Click 1: the risk of each action is the posterior of the other class. Click 2: the smallest risk is the largest posterior: the minimum-error rule of Video 4.2.

A scanner in a hospital

A scanner gives P(sick | x) = 0.2. Missing a sick patient costs 10. A second test for a healthy patient costs 1. Correct decisions cost 0.

  1. Write the loss table (actions: “say healthy”, “say sick”).
  2. Compute the risk of each action. What do you decide?
  3. Above which value of P(sick | x) do we say sick?

Your turn · answers

A scanner in a hospital

  1. say healthy: 0 if healthy, 10 if sick · say sick: 1 if healthy, 0 if sick.
  2. say healthy: 10 × 0.2 = 2.0 · say sick: 1 × 0.8 = 0.8. Say sick (second test), although the patient is probably healthy.
  3. When 10 P > 1 − P, that is P > 1/11 ≈ 0.091.
Answers. The table: missing a sick patient costs 10, a second test costs 1. Risks: 10 times 0.2 is 2.0 for saying healthy, 1 times 0.8 is 0.8 for saying sick: send for the second test. Threshold: 1/11, about 0.091. Next video: more than two classes, and one score per class.
