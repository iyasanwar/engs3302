Where are the mistakes? Look at each side of the boundary.

Left of x* we say salmon. The blue area there is the sea bass that we call salmon: 30.6 in 1000 fish.

The total error is the two areas together: about 65 mistakes in 1000 fish, instead of 400 without a camera.

How the prior moves the boundary

More sea bass, more room for sea bass

careful

Halfway at 5.5 only because both curves have the same width (σ = 1). With different widths: Week 5.

Each boundary is where the posterior of sea bass crosses 0.5. Change only the prior.

20% sea bass: the boundary moves right, to 5.96. Salmon get more of the middle.

Equal priors: the boundary is halfway between the centres, 5.5.

80% sea bass: it moves left, to 5.04. The more common class gets more of the axis.

Why? With more sea bass in the catch, a fish in the middle is more often a sea bass.

Each boundary is where P(sea bass | x) crosses 0.5. Click 1: 20%: 5.96. Click 2: 50%: 5.50, halfway. Click 3: 80%: 5.04. Click 4: with more sea bass, a fish in the middle is more often a sea bass, so we give the middle to sea bass. Careful: halfway at equal priors only because both curves have σ = 1.

A day with 80% sea bass

  1. Left, to 5.04: sea bass are more common, so more of the middle goes to them.
  2. 0.0632 > 0.0388: sea bass (posterior 0.619). With 60% sea bass this fish was a salmon.

Answers. One: left, to 5.04. Two: 0.0632 against 0.0388: sea bass, posterior 0.619; on a 60% day it was salmon. Three: 49.8 mistakes; the lower curve is lower. Next video: when some mistakes cost more than others.
