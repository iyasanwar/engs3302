Now.So.Next.

The extreme tilt: a feature and its exact copy. A fish we know from Week 4: x = 5.4, given twice.

Once: sea bass wins, posterior 0.526, as in Week 4.

Twice: the posterior falls to 0.452, and the decision flips to salmon. Nothing new was measured.

Correlated features do the same, but less: shared evidence counts twice and pushes the posteriors too far.

Two classes, priors 0.5 and 0.5. Class A: p(x₁ | A) = 0.2, p(x₂ | A) = 0.3. Class B: p(x₁ | B) = 0.4, p(x₂ | B) = 0.1.

  1. Compute both naive Bayes scores and decide.
  2. Which class does x₁ alone choose? Why does the answer change?

Naive Bayes by hand

  1. A: 0.5 × 0.2 × 0.3 = 0.030; B: 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.1 = 0.020. Decide A (posterior 0.6).
  2. x₁ alone: 0.2 against 0.4, so B. The second feature changes the decision.
  3. A: × 0.2 = 0.006; B: × 0.4 = 0.008: now B. No: nothing new was measured, x₁ was counted twice. Drop the copy.✗ trust B: three features✓ drop the copy: A
