Same distance, very different density

r² = z₁² − 2ρ z₁z₂ + z₂²1 − ρ²
no tilt2 and 2

density at A ≈ 16 × B

r² of the points: the distance² in units of the cloud

Now.So.Next.

Two new penguins, each one standard deviation from the mean in both features. Which is more typical?

After z-scores, as in Week 2, both are √2 = 1.41 away. Without the tilt they look equally typical.

Units of the cloud: a step along the tilt is cheap, against it costly. A: r² = 1.18. r² = squared Mahalanobis distance

B, long flippers but light, gets r² = 6.67. That is rare: A is about 16 times as dense.

C, two standard deviations in flipper only: r² = 7.84. Points on one ellipse have equal density.

the same z-score distance is not the same density. The tilt decides.

The two directions of the cloud

Σv = λv

eigenvector v: a direction the matrix does not turn. It only makes it longer or shorter. Here: an axis of the ellipse.

eigenvalue λ: how much longer. Here: the variance of the cloud along v.

In z-scores, Σ is the correlation matrix R = [1 ρ; ρ 1]. Its eigenvalues are 1 + ρ and 1 − ρ.

alongλ₁ = 1.70
acrossλ₂ = 0.30
A · 2 / λ₁1.18
B · 2 / λ₂6.67

the spread of the cloud along each axis

Now.So.Next.

Every tilted cloud has two special directions: its eigenvectors. They are the axes of the ellipse.

Along the tilt the spread is λ₁ = 1 + ρ = 1.70. The cloud is long here.

Across the tilt it is only λ₂ = 1 − ρ = 0.30. The cloud is narrow here.

Both points have z₁² + z₂² = 2, but Mahalanobis divides by λ: r² is 1.18 for A, 6.67 for B.

an eigenvalue is the spread of the cloud along one axis. A narrow axis makes distance costly.

Now.So.Next.

A and B are both 1.41 from the centre by ruler. In a tilted cloud the ruler cannot be trusted.

Turn the page until the axes of the cloud lie flat. A turn changes no distance: still 1.41 and 1.41.

Divide each axis by √λ: squeeze the long one, stretch the narrow one. The cloud turns round.

after whitening the ruler gives the Mahalanobis distance r: A 1.08, B 2.58. Squared: 1.18 and 6.67.

Covariance and tilt

  1. Mean (3, 3). Deviations (−1, −2), (1, −1), (0, 3); variances 0.667 and 4.667; products 2, −1, 0: covariance 0.333.
  2. ρ = 0.333 / √(0.667 × 4.667) = 0.19: a weak upward tilt.
