The fish at 5.4 again. Its posteriors: 0.526 sea bass, 0.474 salmon. We must say one word.

If we say sea bass, we are wrong exactly when the fish is a salmon: 0.474.

If we say salmon, we are wrong when it is a sea bass: 0.526.

Sea bass is wrong less often. The rule picks the larger posterior. It does not promise to be right; it is wrong as rarely as possible.

Why it works

Why the larger posterior makes the fewest mistakes

the condition

True when the priors and the curves are correct. With curves fitted to data, a better model can beat ours.

The two posteriors for every lightness x. Where they cross, both are 0.5.

At every x we have two choices. Each choice is wrong with the posterior of the other class.

Choosing the larger posterior always leaves the smaller one as the error: the lower curve, at every x.

Any other rule takes the higher curve somewhere, so it adds mistakes there and saves none anywhere.

The total is the sum over all fish. Each part is as small as possible, so the sum is too: no rule makes fewer mistakes.

The short reason, in five lines. Click 1: at x = 5.4 each choice is wrong with the other posterior; the rule takes 0.474. Click 2: at every x the error is the lower curve. Click 3: another rule, with its line at 6, says salmon between 5.36 and 6; there it takes the upper curve: more mistakes. Click 4: the total is the sum over all fish, so it is the smallest. The condition: our priors and densities must be correct. With fitted curves, Week 5 and later can do better.

The rule in one line

The shortcut and the name

decide ω₁ if P(ω₁ | x) > P(ω₂ | x)same as: p(x | ω₁) P(ω₁) > p(x | ω₂) P(ω₂)P(error | x) = min[P(ω₁ | x), P(ω₂ | x)] c classes: 1 − max
scores at 5.4:0.06660.0599
compare the scores (step 1 only)0.0666>0.0599→sea bass
its names

Bayes decision rule, or MAP rule: maximum a posteriori, “the largest posterior”.

The evidence p(x) is the same for both classes. Dividing by it never changes which one is larger.

So compare the two scores directly: 0.0666 > 0.0599. Sea bass, with less work.

Many books and tools call this the MAP rule. Remember: it chooses a class.

The chance that the decision is wrong is the smaller posterior. With three or more classes: 1 minus the largest.

The rule: the larger posterior. The evidence is the same for both classes, so we can compare likelihood times prior directly. Click 1: 0.0666 > 0.0599: sea bass. Click 2: the names Bayes decision rule and MAP rule, maximum a posteriori. Click 3: P(error | x) is the smaller posterior; with c classes it is 1 minus the largest.
